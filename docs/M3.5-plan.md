# M3.5 plan: Asset pipeline test

Proposed 2026-09-28, at the owner's request: test the asset workflow in `docs/assets.md` end to end on a
few pilot assets before M4 builds enemies and a boss with it. Placed between M3 (Magic) and M4 (Tripo
enemies); step 2 can start any time, even during M3, since it only needs the owner's tools.

**Goal:** prove that an image made in any tool, with the house style reference, becomes a model, texture or
icon in the deployed game without hand-fixing, within budget, at 60 fps, and looking like one set.

**Done when:** the pilot assets (an urn, a pedestal, the Ember Sigil's model, the crypt floor texture and
three artifact icons) are in the deployed game through the pipeline, each with a working fallback to its
placeholder; they fit their budgets and a room full of them holds 60 fps; the owner has accepted the house
style and each asset; and `docs/assets.md`, the Tripo checklist and `CREDITS.md` are updated with what the
pilot taught.

Each step lists who does it (**owner** or **Claude**) and the checks that close it. A check is done when
both agree.

## Step 1: Tooling (Claude, built 2026-09-29)

- [x] `npm run prepare-asset -- <file.glb> --kind <hero|enemy|boss|prop|large-prop|artifact> --id <id>`: budgets
      per kind in `data/assets.json`; checks in `tools/assetReport.ts` that pass, warn, fail or were fixed
      (centre and base moved, a character facing -Z turned from its hand bones, triangles lowered with
      meshoptimizer, textures shrunk, PNG kept where a material is transparent); units, proportions, rig,
      small textures, round base, clips and roles checked; `--check` only reports
- [x] `npm run prepare-texture -- <file> --kind <floor|wall|icon> --id <id>`: resize and compress; tiles get a
      seam score, a light spread and a 2 × 2 preview; icons a warning without a transparent background and a
      sheet at 28 and 44 px, plain, cursed and cleansed, on the game's dark background (`asset-previews/`)
- [x] `data/assets.json` (budgets; `props`, `artifacts`, `floors`, `icons`) read by the pure
      `src/assets/assetData.ts`, unit-tested: every id known, heights positive, paths inside the assets folder,
      every file there
- [x] `src/assets/models.ts` loads them at boot before the first floor is built; each model stood on the floor
      at its height and shared by all its uses; a missing or broken file keeps its placeholder, with a warning
- [x] Hooks: urns (the sealed one the same model tinted, its ward fitted round it), pedestals, an artifact's
      floating model, a floor look's painted tile (with the stains and bones drawn over it), an artifact's
      icon (HUD, lore card, inventory, end screen)
- [x] Dev menu, Made assets: the list with status and triangles, "Made assets: on/off" (this floor built again
      on its seed, back in the same room), "Show them here" (urns and a pedestal ahead of the mage), and the
      frame readout (frame rate, draw calls, triangles); `?assets=off`, and `?assets=<manifest>.json`
- [x] Browser tests: the stand-ins load and replace their placeholders and turn off; missing and broken files
      and `?assets=off` leave the placeholders with warnings and no errors

**Checks:** a stand-in model (a Tripo test object, or a free CC0 model) goes through the whole pipeline to a
room; the report catches a model that is too heavy, too small, off-centre or facing the wrong way; removing
the file brings the placeholder back.

**How they went (2026-09-29):**
- `tools/make-standins.ts` makes stand-ins in code (an urn made off-centre and too heavy, a pedestal, a knotted
  sigil, a floor tile, an icon) and sends them through both scripts into `tests/fixtures/assets/`. The urn
  came out centred and lowered from about 6,000 to 1,924 triangles, and the sigil was lifted onto y = 0. The
  seam check caught that the stand-in floor doesn't tile.
- Unit tests build models that fail on purpose: too heavy, a thousand times too small, off-centre, facing
  away, too wide, a character with no rig.
- The browser tests load the stand-ins through their manifest (`?assets=/tests/fixtures/assets/assets.json`),
  and a manifest with missing and broken files.
- The play-test hero passes every check.

## Step 2: House style (owner)

**First, the look (2026-09-29).** The owner's samples mix pixel art (a wall torch, a brazier, a lightning bolt) with
a clean cartoon chibi mage, and the owner is drawn to a cartoony or pixel style for the high fantasy and the magic.
Claude proposed pixel-art 3D:
- Tripo models and the game's lights stay, drawn into a small target and blown up in whole pixels, with outlines;
- tiles, icons and effects are drawn as pixel art;
- concept art for Tripo stays clean cartoon.

The alternative is cartoon 3D at full resolution. To choose in the game itself, the dev menu has a **pixel look**
switch (`src/render/pixelLook.ts`, tuning in `CFG.pixelLook`, kept in the browser; `?pixel=270` or `off`):
- 360, 270 or 180 lines, the camera snapped to the pixel grid;
- outlines from depth;
- light in bands.

**Decided (2026-09-30):** pixel-art 3D. The game starts in it at 360 lines (270 at first; the play-test that day found the
mage and small things unreadable full-screen), with outlines and without light in bands
(`CFG.pixelLook.start`; the dev menu and `?pixel=` still switch it). The GDD's art direction and the house style in
`docs/assets.md` say so now. The house style reference below is chosen to match.

- [ ] One to three reference images (or a MidJourney `--sref` code) that are the house style, recorded in
      `docs/assets.md` (Style references)
- [ ] A style test sheet: the urn from the same prompt with the reference, four variations in MidJourney and
      four in one other image model (Tripo's or another)
- [ ] Claude puts the sheet next to game screenshots of the crypt, drowned and burning floors

**Checks:** the variations from both tools look like one set; they read in the torchlight of all
three floors; colours stay muted and the light even. If one tool drifts, note how to prompt it or drop it.

## Step 3: Pilot models (owner makes, Claude brings in)

- [ ] Urn: small repeated prop, up to 2,000 triangles, plain and rune-sealed from one model
- [ ] Pedestal: small prop under every artifact
- [ ] Ember Sigil: an artifact model floating over its pedestal, turning

**Checks:**
- the `prepare-asset` report passes for all three (or says exactly what to change in Tripo)
- screenshots of each in the crypt, drowned and burning looks, next to the placeholder it replaces
- a room with eight urns, two pedestals and an artifact holds 60 fps in the dev menu's readout, on the
  owner's machine and on integrated graphics if one is at hand
- readable from the game's camera: the urn's silhouette, the sigil's shape at 30 pixels
- the urn still breaks with its burst, the sealed one still shows its ward, the pedestal still blocks
- scale and placement right: nothing floats, sinks or clips into walls
- with the file removed, the placeholder is back

## Step 4: Pilot textures and icons (owner makes, Claude brings in)

- [ ] Crypt floor: a seamless tile for floor 1
- [ ] Icons for the Ember Sigil, the Bone Lens and the Mask of the Drowned: one painted, and one rendered from
      the Ember Sigil model, to compare the two ways

**Checks:**
- no visible seams in the 2 × 2 preview or in a large hall
- the game's cracks, stains and bones still read on the new floor, and the floor doesn't outshine the props
- each icon reads at 28 px on the HUD and 44 px on the lore card, with the red ring on the cursed mask
- painted against rendered: which reads better, and which takes less work
