# M3.5 plan: The look (asset pipeline, pixel look, room kit)

Proposed 2026-09-28, at the owner's request, as a test of the asset workflow in `docs/assets.md` on a few pilot
assets. Changed 2026-09-30: the owner chose the pixel look, and code over Tripo for everything but special models (the
mage, and bosses where wanted), so the pilot became the room kit built in code.

**Goal:** one look for the whole game: the pixel look, and every piece of a room built in code in the style of the spell
effects, within budget at 60 fps; the asset pipeline ready for the special models that still come from Tripo.

**Done when:** the pipeline's tooling is built (step 1, done); the pixel look is chosen (step 2, done: 540 lines, 1080 since the proof's play-test); the
room kit is redone in code and accepted by the owner, look by look (step 3); and `docs/assets.md` and `docs/effects.md`
say how new pieces are built (step 4).

Each step lists who does it (**owner** or **Claude**) and the checks that close it. A check is done when
both agree.

- [x] `src/assets/models.ts` loads them at boot before the first floor is built; each model stood on the floor

**Checks:** a stand-in model (a Tripo test object, or a free CC0 model) goes through the whole pipeline to a
room; the report catches a model that is too heavy, too small, off-centre or facing the wrong way; removing
the file brings the placeholder back.

**How they went (2026-09-29):**
- `tools/make-standins.ts` makes stand-ins in code (an urn made off-centre and too heavy, a pedestal, a knotted
  sigil, a floor tile, an icon) and sends them through both scripts into `tests/fixtures/assets/`. The urn
  came out centred and lowered from about 6,000 to 1,924 triangles, and the sigil was lifted onto y = 0. The
  seam check caught that the stand-in floor doesn't tile.
- Unit tests build models that fail on purpose: too heavy, a thousand times too small, off-centre, facing
  away, too wide, a character with no rig.
- The browser tests load the stand-ins through their manifest (`?assets=/tests/fixtures/assets/assets.json`),
  and a manifest with missing and broken files.
- The play-test hero passes every check.

## Step 2: The look (both, decided 2026-09-30)

**First, the look (2026-09-29).** The owner's samples mix pixel art (a wall torch, a brazier, a lightning bolt) with
a clean cartoon chibi mage, and the owner is drawn to a cartoony or pixel style for the high fantasy and the magic.
Claude proposed pixel-art 3D:
- Tripo models and the game's lights stay, drawn into a small target and blown up in whole pixels, with outlines;
- tiles, icons and effects are drawn as pixel art;
- concept art for Tripo stays clean cartoon.

The alternative is cartoon 3D at full resolution. To choose in the game itself, the dev menu has a **pixel look**
switch (`src/render/pixelLook.ts`, tuning in `CFG.pixelLook`, kept in the browser; `?pixel=270` or `off`):
- 360, 270 or 180 lines, the camera snapped to the pixel grid;
- outlines from depth;
- light in bands.

**Decided (2026-09-30):** pixel-art 3D. The game starts in it at 540 lines (270 at first, then 360; the play-tests that day
found the mage and small things unreadable full-screen on a high-resolution monitor), with outlines and without light in bands
(`CFG.pixelLook.start`; the dev menu and `?pixel=` still switch it). The GDD's art direction and the house style in
`docs/assets.md` say so now. After playing the room kit's proof, the owner set the default to 1080 lines (2026-09-30).

**Decided (2026-09-30): no Tripo pilot.** The owner is happy with the models and textures built in code, so the
house style for images and the Tripo pilot (the old steps 2 to 5: a style reference and test sheet, an urn, a
pedestal and the Ember Sigil from Tripo, a painted floor tile and icons) are dropped. Everything in the game is built in
code, in the style of the spell effects (`docs/effects.md`). Tripo, and this guide's pipeline for it, stay for special
models: the mage, and bosses where wanted. The tooling of step 1 stays for those.

**How the proof is built (2026-09-30):**
- **Fire** (`src/render/fire.ts`): a flame is a cream core painted over a sheath of its colour, with a faint red edge of
  added light, tongues beside it and embers rising. The GPU moves it all from one clock: flames lick, flicker and sway,
  each on its own beat, and embers rise and shrink. Colours are baked into each fire's geometry, so every fire in the
  game shares four materials and draws in three or four calls. The sheath is painted, not added, so a flame keeps its
  colours in front of a torch-lit wall (added light went white there).
- **The fire objects** (`src/world/fireLooks.ts`):
  - the wall torch in each floor's style: an iron bracket with a pitch torch (crypt; a warding rune on the bracket was dropped: the torch hid it), a cage lantern
    whose glass glows (drowned), a brazier on a bracket (burning);
  - the brazier: splayed legs with claw feet, a collar, a deep bowl of heaped coals under a bronze rim, three bronze
    plaques whose runes kindle with it;
  - the candle stand: a clawed foot, a pole with two knops, a dish with a bronze rim, three to five candles of different
    heights with wax running down them.
  - Torches stand upright and close to the wall: seen from 45° above, whatever leans out towards the camera drops down
    the screen (the first torch's flame showed below its bracket).
  - Their still pieces are painted (`paint` in `render/materials.ts`): `mergeStill` bakes each piece's colour into its
    vertices, so an object's body draws in one call whatever it is made of.
- **The crypt's floor and walls** (`src/render/pixelTextures.ts`): drawn texel by texel at 48 × 34 texels a unit
  (`CFG.roomKit.texel`). The floor seen from 45° above is shorter down the screen than across, so the texels are too; on
  screen they come out square, about 1.3 of the pixel look's pixels at 540 lines and one in the great halls. The
  textures are sampled nearest up close.
  - Slabs (most a tile, some two or four), bevelled, mottled, cracked and chipped.
  - Stains in ordered dither, and bones and skulls as little sprites.
  - The edges darkened towards the walls in dithered steps.
  - The special rooms' marks hardened into whole texels, and bevelled bricks with chips and cracks.
- **The switch:** the dev menu's Look tab ("Room kit: new / old", and "Fire objects here"), `?kit=old`,
  `__game.roomKit` and `__game.useRoomKit(on)`. Other looks keep their old floors and walls until the proof is accepted;
  every floor's wall lights, braziers and candle stands use the new fire.
- **The cost:** 8 to 12 draw calls more than the old look in a one-cell room at rest (137 against 129 in the first
  room). The great hall is level (185 against 184).

**Checks:**
- screenshots of each in the crypt, drowned and burning looks at 540 lines, next to the old look
- readable from the game's camera: silhouettes and states (lit, charged, broken) at a glance
- a room packed with objects stays within its draw calls (the great hall test) and holds 60 fps in the dev menu's readout
- what each piece does is unchanged: breaking, blocking, lighting, charging, ringing; the browser tests pass
- the floors: no visible seams in a large hall, and the cracks, stains and bones drawn over them still read
