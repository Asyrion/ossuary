Scene 1: Early Morning Preparations

- It is 'Tarakus Blessing Day,' an annual Huruhui festival honoring their loyal birdlike partners.
- Tarakus are massive bipedal creatures with scaled legs and sleek feathered crests, prized for hauling goods and traveling long distances across forest trails.
- Villagers gather special flowers to weave garlands for the Tarakus' harnesses.
- Azyrion wakes early to help his mother Ylani harvest pale blue blossoms reserved for the ceremony.
- He grumbles about wearing his own flower wreath, which keeps slipping over his eyes.

Scene 2: Decorating the Tarakus

- Children and elders gather in the village clearing where the Tarakus wait, tall and alert, feathers shining with early dew.
- Azyrion is assigned to an older Tarakus named 'Moro,' recognizable by its dignified striped crest and calm yellow eyes.
- He carefully drapes woven garlands over Moro's harness and hornlike brow plates, whispering thanks while the creature tilts its head, blinking slowly.
- Elder Kaien instructs the children to speak words of gratitude and promise to care for the land that feeds them all.

Scene 3: Procession Through the Village

- The decorated Tarakus lead a slow, rhythmic procession along mossy forest paths.
- Villagers sing ancient Huruhui chants, their voices rising in harmony with birdsong.
- Children scatter herbs along the path while the Tarakus stride proudly, garlands fluttering in the breeze.
- Azyrion walks beside Moro, slipping him a piece of sweet root when the elders aren't looking, making the old beast let out a soft warbling call.

Scene 4: Evening Feast and Stories

- As dusk settles, cooking fires warm the gathering.
- Roots, greens, and smoked game are shared on woven mats beneath vine-wrapped wooden lanterns.
- Elders tell the old story of when the Huruhui first befriended the Tarakus-once wild and untamed, now loyal partners because humans chose respect over domination.
- Azyrion listens, sleepy-eyed, resting against Moro's warm flank as the birdlike creature settles down, guarding the circle with serene, unblinking calm.
