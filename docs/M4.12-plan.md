# M4.12 Fantasy foes: plan

The owner's ask (2026-10-02): "There are still some cute enemy designs in the game. I'd like them to be more fantasy
oriented. The Mantis and the moth are just two examples. Create a milestone for that."

Proposed 2026-10-02 and **approved the same day** with every recommendation (Decisions, below): all forty-six, the
concepts as written, names following the creature, built after M6 step 7 and before step 8, and the Hollow Warden's
three looks all redone, one chosen then. Nothing is built yet.

## What the game has now

Every look was stood in the Galleries tab and looked at (2026-10-02): the fifty-nine looks the foes wear and the eleven
boss looks fall in three groups.

**Cute (32): round, with big googly eyes.** The ten foes of M4 and the eight of M4.1 were drawn before M4.5's redesign rules
and never redone: round bodies, big white eyes from `bigEyes`, smaller than the mage (the Sporeling, the Vesper Moth, the
Rune Beetle and the rest). They are the commonest foes on floors 1 and 2 (the Sporeling, Vesper Moth and Rune Beetle carry
the heaviest weights), so every run meets them first. The six Wardlings are googly-eyed orbs. Eight boss looks are the same
kind: the Hollow Warden's three (the Bell Warden's eyes under its rim, the Mother of Spores' smiling face, the Hollow
Owl), the Hallowed Hart's fawn face, the Deep Angler's cartoon eyes, the War-Tiger's puppy face, the Pillar Dragon's head
and the jelly serpent.

**Plain animals (14): not cute, but not fantasy either.** M4.5's redesign made its thirty-five fierce and readable, but
fourteen came out as the real animal with a school's colour: the Storm Mantis is a green praying mantis, the Storm Darter
a dragonfly, the Ember Wasps wasps. The others: the Crypt Spider, Ink Octopus, Firefly Swarm, Choir Lark, Rubble Crab, Rime
Snail, Glass Newt, Rime Scarab, Gloom Raven, Rime Wolf and Dust Pangolin.

**Fantasy already (21), kept:** the Grimoire Mimic, Brine Serpent, Puddle Elemental, Thunderhead, Puffcap, Cinder Djinn,
Magma Ooze, Phoenix, Basilisk, Salt Golem, Rubble Golem, Griffin, Radiant Eye, Harpy, Lodestone Golem, Hearth Golem, Shadow
Fox, Frost Jackalope, Glacier Golem, Hydra and Manticore; and the bosses the Drowned Abbess, Pyre Knight and Twin Guardians.

## The rules

M4.5's six rules still hold (readable at a glance; fierce, not cute: no big round eyes, no smiles, eyes small glowing slits
under a brow; their real size; no undead; not cute in name; the owner sees the looks before they replace the old). Five
more:

7. **Fantasy, not natural history.** Every foe is a creature that could only live in Eda: a creature of the old bestiaries
   (kelpie, mandrake, unicorn, kobold, caladrius, tarasque, dragon turtle) or an animal magic has changed past anything
   natural (horns, plate-like armour, extra eyes, crystal or rune growths, flame or frost in its body). No plain animal in
   a school's colour.
8. **The school is in the body.** Runes cut into horn, crystal growing from the spine, flame for a mane, lightning in a
   hollow bell: the school is part of the creature, not a tint.
9. **The same fight.** Behaviours, data, counters, tuning and ids stay; only the look and the display name change. Each new
   look keeps what its behaviour moves (its `parts` by name: the Radiant Eye's `lidTop`, the Hydra's `neck0` to `neck4`, a
   boss's `eye`, `ring` and `halo`, the War-Tiger's `plate0` to `plate3`...), its hidden states and roughly its size (the
   foe's `size` is its hit circle; the look must not promise more or less).
10. **Judged as played.** Each look is judged in the texel look at play distance, where a foe is a few dozen game pixels,
    as well as close up: the silhouette and the slit eyes must read at that size.
11. **Within budget.** About 15 draw calls after `mergeRig`, triangles near today's, colours in the vertices, stepped glows.

## Steps

1. **The tools.** Each new look lands first as a gallery option beside the old one, so the game is unchanged until the
   owner says yes; the Galleries tab shows old and new side by side, and a switch (`DEV.foeLooks`, `?looks=old`) dresses
   the foes in either. A unit test builds every look and checks it keeps the parts its behaviour moves and a height near
   its size. A contact sheet script (`npm run inspect -- foes`) shoots every look in the texel look at play distance and
   close up, for the owner.
   *Built (2026-10-02):* `src/actors/fantasyLooks.ts` (`FANTASY_LOOKS` by the look id each replaces, `APPROVED`,
   `fantasyLook` read by `buildFoe` through `oldLook`), `DEV.foeLooks` (approved, new or old; the Look tab's switch builds
   the floor again, `?looks=new`), `src/actors/lookPairs.ts` (`showLookPairs`: old left, new right, a floor at a time, the
   Galleries tab; `lookContract`: the parts each new look lacks and both heights, held by a browser test), and the contact
   sheets as their own script, `npm run foe-sheet -- floor=1` (the pairs in the pixel look and close up, the contract
   printed), rather than a mode of `inspect`.
2. **Floor 1's ten.**
   *Built (2026-10-02), waiting on the owner's yes:* `src/actors/fantasyFloor1.ts`: the Spore Fiend (a horned fungal beast,
   its hood torn into points, spore sacs glowing violet), the Seraph Moth (six wings, each with an eye of pale fire), the
   Runehorn (a basalt ram with pale rune-cut horns, a ridge of storm crystals and plates that part), the Urn Mimic (fangs
   round its rim and lid, ember slits), the Gloom Weaver, the Quill Kraken, the Lampwyrms (small lantern-throated wyrms), the
   Caladrius (white and gold, a halo crest), the Mantis Knight (plate, a crested helm, crystal scythes) and the Helm Hermit
   (a stone helm for a shell, a storm eye in its holes); each keeps its old look's parts. The sheets (`npm run foe-sheet --
   floor=1`, `only=` for a few) crop round each pair: the pixel look's pixels blown up whole, and a close-up with the look
   off. A browser test spawns each foe in its new look and lets it fight a while.
3. **Floor 2's six.**
4. **Floor 3's four** (and the nest).
5. **Floor 4's five.**
6. **Floor 5's seven.**
7. **The Wardlings.**
8. **The bosses' eight looks.**
9. **The close:** the names through `STR`, the reviews, the GDD as built, a sweep of the rooms, `npm run balance` to
   confirm nothing moved, the owner's play-test.

Steps 2 to 8 each end with the contact sheet for the owner; a floor's foes switch to their new looks on the owner's yes,
and the old looks are deleted then.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Scope.** All forty-six (the 32 cute ones and the 14 plain animals)? Recommended: yes, all of them, so no plain or cute
   creature is left beside the fantasy ones.
2. **The concepts.** Veto or replace any in the tables above. Recommended as written: each keeps its fight readable (a
   ram still charges, a kobold still runs cover to cover with its candle).
3. **Names follow the creature** (the Moonstag Fawn becomes the Pale Unicorn). Ids stay, so the bestiary kept in the
   browser and the tests keep working; names stay placeholders until M8. Recommended: yes.
4. **When.** M6 step 7 (runes) is half built. Recommended: finish step 7, then build M4.12 before M6 step 8, since the
   commonest foes on floors 1 and 2 are the cute ones and every play-test meets them first.
5. **The Hollow Warden.** Recommended: redo all three looks and choose one then; or name the one to keep now and only it
   is redone.
